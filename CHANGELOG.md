# Changelog

All notable changes to Curio, newest first. The version number is also shown in the app.

## 1.4.0 - 2026-10-30
- Home screen reorganised: lessons you have started appear at the top under "Continue" with a progress bar, finished lessons move to a "Completed" section at the bottom, and new lessons stay in their topics in between.
- Opening a lesson you left halfway takes you back to the card where you stopped.
- 5 new lessons, 36 in total: the Apollo 11 landing, the ocean, the Industrial Revolution, your brain in numbers, and DNA and the genome. Figures checked against sources.

## 1.3.1 - 2026-10-30
- Once the app is installed, the install button turns into "Uninstall app" and shows how to remove it (browsers do not let a web app remove itself, so it shows the steps).

## 1.3.0 - 2026-10-29
- 7 new lessons, 31 in total: moving continents, the Black Death, Athenian democracy, our solar system, how batteries work, the web is not the internet, and why fees matter.
- Fact-check pass on the 1.2.0 lessons against sources. Corrected: Great Pyramid build time and who built it, Pantheon dome date and width, CO2 level (about 430 ppm), warming since 1850 to 1900 (about 1.4°C in 2025), and a few smaller wording issues.

## 1.2.0 - 2026-10-29
- 8 more lessons, 24 in total: your immune system, how vaccines work, the greenhouse effect, the Roman Empire, the Great Pyramid, how GPS works, transistors and chips, and probability traps.

## 1.1.0 - 2026-10-28
- 8 new lessons, 16 in total: black holes, the science of sleep, how evolution works, how encryption keeps secrets, the printing press, the Silk Road, why languages change, and inflation.
- Key-term cards now show the term and its meaning; every lesson ends with a short recap.

## 1.0.0 - 2026-10-28
- First release: a swipeable lesson feed with quick questions.
- 8 lessons in 4 topics: space, the human body, how money grows, thinking like an economist, five turning points in history, ways of thinking, how AI chatbots work, and how the internet works.
- Card types: short facts with a key number, multiple-choice questions with explanations, "guess first" number estimates, and a compounding simulator.
- Quick mix: a random set of 12 cards from every lesson.
- Daily streak and completed-lesson scores, saved on your device.
- Installable as an app, works offline, shows where it is hosted.
